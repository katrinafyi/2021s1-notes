**Definition.** $(S, *)$ has an identity element if there exists $e \in S$ such that for all $a \in S$, $a * e = e * a = a$.

**Theorem.** If there exists an identity, then it is unique.

**Proof.** Let $e, e'$ be identity elements for $(S, *)$. Then, since $e$ is an identity we have $e * e' = e'$. Since $e'$ is also an identity, we have $e' * e = e$ and so, $e = e'$ and it is unique.

For example, 1 is the identity for $(\mathbb Q, \cdot)$ and $(\mathbb R, \cdot)$, and 0 is the identity for $(\mathbb Q, +)$, and $(\mathbb R, +)$. The identity function is the identity for $(\mathcal F_A, \circ)$.

As a non-example, $(\mathbb N, +)$ has no identity.
