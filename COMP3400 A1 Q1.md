$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname*{True} &= \lambda x. \lambda y.x \\ 
\operatorname*{False}& = \lambda x. \lambda y.y \\ 
\operatorname*{AND} &=\lambda p.\lambda q.((pq)p) \\ 
\operatorname*{OR} &=\lambda p.\lambda q.((pp)q) \\ 
\end{aligned}
$$
Here, we will reduce $(\operatorname*{AND} \operatorname*{True}) \operatorname*{False}$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname*{AND} \operatorname*{True}) \operatorname*{False} 
&=(\lambda p.\lambda q.(pq)p)\ (\lambda x.\lambda y.x) \operatorname*{False} && \text{by definition}\\ 
&\to (\lambda q.(pq)p)[p:=\lambda x.\lambda y.x] \operatorname*{False} &&\text{beta reduction}\\ 
&= (\lambda q.(\lambda x.\lambda y.x)(q)(\lambda x.\lambda y.x)) \operatorname*{False} &&\text{binding} \\ 
&\to (\lambda q.(\lambda y.x)[x:=q](\lambda x.\lambda y.x)) \operatorname*{False} &&\text{beta reduction} \\ 
&= (\lambda q.(\lambda y.q)(\lambda x.\lambda y.x)) \operatorname*{False} &&\text{binding} \\ 
&\to (\lambda q.(q)[y:=\lambda x.\lambda y.x]) \operatorname*{False} &&\text{beta-reduction} \\ 
&= (\lambda q.q) \operatorname*{False} &&\text{binding} \\ 
&\to (q)[q:= \operatorname*{False}] &&\text{beta-reduction} \\  
&= \operatorname{False}
\end{aligned}
$$
So the expression reduces to $\operatorname*{False}$. This is actually the case regardless of how $\operatorname*{False}$ is defined (we didn't need to expand it).
